The RMS Titanic was a British ocean liner that sank in the North Atlantic Ocean 
on 15 April 1912 after striking an iceberg during her maiden voyage from 
Southampton to New York City.

Of the estimated 2,224 passengers and crew aboard, more than 1,500 died, 
making it the deadliest sinking of a single ship up to that time.

The ship was operated by the White Star Line and constructed at the Harland 
and Wolff shipyard in Belfast. She was the largest ship afloat at the time 
she entered service.

First-class passengers had access to a gymnasium, swimming pool, libraries, 
restaurants, and cafes. Third-class passengers had far fewer amenities.

Women and children were given priority access to lifeboats. The ship carried 
only 20 lifeboats, which was not enough for everyone on board.

The wreck of the Titanic was discovered in 1985 by Robert Ballard at a depth 
of about 3,800 metres off the coast of Newfoundland.
